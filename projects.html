Vision-Based Unmanned Aerial Manipulator Control

Prof. Lin Zhao, CORE Lab, National University of Singapore

A quadrotor carrying a three degree of freedom arm has to grasp a moving object while the arm it is carrying disturbs its own dynamics. Several 6D pose estimators were compared, including FoundationPose, SAM-6D, EfficientPose and YOLOv5-6D, and FoundationPose was deployed for real-time tracking from RGB-D. Tracking during high-speed pick-up used a hybrid model predictive and L1 adaptive controller, the adaptive part absorbing the model parameter uncertainty the manipulator introduces. Closed-loop grasping was demonstrated in PX4-Gazebo and on hardware.

Fault-Tolerant Control under Rotor Failure

Inter-IIT Tech Meet, 2024 · second place among all IITs

A quadrotor that loses a rotor cannot hold yaw, and the standard controller fights the asymmetry until the vehicle tumbles. The fix is to accept the spin and stabilise around it. Detection runs on the onboard IMU and GPS alone, with a modified two-stage Kalman filter adapting the control effectiveness matrix in thirty milliseconds, and nonlinear dynamic inversion handling the reconfigured vehicle. Everything runs on the PX4 stack with no external computer and no added sensors.

Deriving the controller turned up an error in the proof underlying the reference paper by Sun and Scaramuzza. We rewrote the proof and the correction was confirmed by the original author.

Point Cloud Generation from Raw Data

Prof. Leena Vachhani, IIT Bombay · paper under review

A planner does not need every point a sensor returns, it needs the handful of vehicles that could actually matter in the next few seconds. A grid segmentation algorithm selects exactly those, and was benchmarked against ball trees, approximate nearest neighbours and k-d trees. Computation time falls to 0.193 seconds per timestep, which is what makes it usable inside a real-time loop rather than as offline preprocessing.

Adversarial Training for Ergodic Reward

Prof. Dominik Baumann, Cyber-Physical Systems Group, Aalto University

Standard reinforcement learning maximises expected reward, which quietly assumes the average over time and the average over many parallel runs are the same thing. In non-ergodic environments they are not, and an agent optimising the wrong average can be confidently wrong. This work uses a Wasserstein GAN to transform the reward so existing algorithms apply unchanged. A generator transforms the time-average rewards and a discriminator evaluates them against ensemble averages, with discriminator accuracy near one half indicating the two have become indistinguishable.

Thanopod, Two-Wheeled Self-Balancing Robot

Team genSUS, IIT Bombay · Robofest Gujarat 4.0, GUJCOST

A two-wheeled robot is an inverted pendulum that has to stay upright while also going somewhere, and the two goals conflict. Driving forward requires first falling forward. The solution is a cascade, with an outer loop deciding how far to lean and a fast inner loop holding that lean. Motor dynamics were simulated to model the nonlinear plant, an LQR controller designed for stability, and a Stanley controller with computer vision handled line tracking on a curvilinear course.

Robotics R&D Intern, ABB Innovation Centre

Robotics Lab, Bangalore · May to July 2024

Palletising is two-dimensional bin packing with physical consequences, because an arrangement that is mathematically optimal is useless if the gripper cannot reach a box or the stack falls over in transit. I built a two-phase recursive partitioning algorithm for single-SKU palletising in .NET C#, and a mixed-SKU pattern generator in four variants accounting for gripper clearance, placement error and stability under motion, with backtracking for feasibility checks.
