Lane-Less Autonomous Driving for Indian Roads

Prof. Parthib Khound, IIT Bombay · funded by Honda Cars India Limited · master's thesis

Dense Indian traffic has no usable lane structure. Vehicles of very different sizes and speeds share a continuous road surface, gaps open and close constantly, and drivers negotiate rather than follow rules. The models that autonomous driving research is built on, such as IDM and MOBIL, were calibrated on traffic that does not behave this way, so they cannot be trusted as a test environment here.

The work has two halves. The first builds a faithful re-simulation of this traffic and validates it against real trajectory data, so that any controller tested inside it is being tested against something real. The second is the planning and control pipeline for the ego vehicle, currently a model predictive controller combined with a repulsive-potential lookahead planner whose formulation borrows the discounting idea from reinforcement learning.

Every vehicle uses a kinematic bicycle model, and clearance between vehicles is computed with exact rotated-rectangle geometry rather than centre to centre distance, which matters when the whole problem is about how narrow a gap a driver will accept.

Fixed-Time Convergent Neural CBF-CLF Control

Prof. Shishir Kolathaya, Stochastic Robotics Lab, IISc Bangalore

A control barrier function is a certificate. It defines a region of the state space that a controller is proved never to leave, which is how you get a safety guarantee rather than a safety hope. The difficulty is that constructing one by hand becomes intractable as the state dimension and the number of obstacles grow.

This work learns the certificate instead. A neural control Lyapunov function represents the minimum time to reach the goal, and a single network learns obstacle avoidance in coordinates taken relative to each obstacle, which is what makes it scale across both dimension and obstacle count. The individual barriers are then combined with a compositional soft minimum. Convergence is fixed time rather than asymptotic, so a manoeuvre completes within a bounded horizon instead of merely approaching the goal forever.

Hybrid Reach-Avoid MPC with Backstepping Control

Prof. Shankar Deka, Nonlinear Systems and Control, Aalto University

Model predictive control over a finite horizon can paint itself into a corner. A sequence of individually reasonable decisions leads to a state from which no feasible solution exists, and the optimiser simply fails. This is not a tuning problem, it is structural.

The controller here integrates exponential control barrier functions into a backstepping design so that feasibility is guaranteed rather than hoped for. The reach-avoid set that the barrier construction produces is what certifies that the optimiser will always have a solution available to it.

Bilevel and Decentralised Optimisation

Prof. Mayank Baranwal, IIT Bombay · Undergraduate Research Award, 2025

A bilevel problem is an optimisation whose constraint is itself an optimisation. The upper level chooses a decision, the lower level responds optimally to it, and the upper level has to account for that response when it differentiates. This structure appears wherever one decision maker anticipates another, which covers hyperparameter selection, Stackelberg games, meta-learning and a great deal of multi-agent control.

The difficulty is the hypergradient. Computing how the upper objective changes with the upper variable requires the sensitivity of the lower solution, which brings in an inverse Hessian that may be singular and a gradient that may be unbounded.

My first line of work addresses this in the continuous setting. Applying the Polyak-Lojasiewicz inequality to the lower-level function gives asymptotic convergence guarantees under minimal assumptions even when the upper level is non-convex, with Tikhonov regularisation handling the singularity and a projection operator keeping gradients bounded.

The second line moves the problem onto a network, where no agent holds the whole objective and agents must agree on a solution while solving it. The optimiser is single loop and combines Chebyshev-accelerated consensus with optimistic gradient tracking. Its main contribution is separating the three sources of cost, the network topology, the outer-level conditioning and the inner-adjoint conditioning, and bounding each independently, which earlier analyses entangle. The method attains the communication lower bound.
