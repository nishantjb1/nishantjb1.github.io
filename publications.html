Network-Optimal Single-Loop Decentralized Bilevel Optimization via Chebyshev-Optimistic Gradient Tracking

With Shikhar Moondra and Mayank Baranwal, IIT Bombay · Undergraduate Research Award

Over a network, the bilevel hypergradient couples three global objects at once, the outer variable, the lower-level solution, and the adjoint solution of a global linear system. Existing decentralised methods pay for this with nested inner loops. Our algorithm updates all of them once per iteration using Chebyshev-accelerated consensus, optimistic outer acceleration and simultaneous gradient tracking, and attains the accelerated network dependence known for single-level decentralised optimisation. Two separate lower bounds show that decentralised bilevel optimisation has two independent hardness mechanisms rather than one.

Finite-Time and Asymptotic Convergence of Gradient-Flow Methods for Bilevel Optimization

Shikhar Moondra*, Nishant Bhave*, Mayank Baranwal · IIT Bombay · *equal contribution

Bilevel algorithms are usually studied in discrete time. This paper treats the problem as a continuous-time gradient flow driven by a hypergradient estimator and analyses it with Lyapunov stability theory, across three regularity regimes. A normalised flow reaches the global optimum in finite time when both levels are strongly convex, a Polyak-Lojasiewicz upper level converges to a neighbourhood sized by the problem conditioning, and a non-convex upper level converges asymptotically under the standard flow.

SPART: A Lightweight 2-D LiDAR Emulation Tool for Trajectory-Dataset-Driven Autonomous Vehicle Testing

With J Veejay Karthik and Leena Vachhani, IIT Bombay

Most traffic datasets carry no sensor measurements, while graphical simulators such as CARLA and Gazebo produce sensor data from scripted behaviour that misses the irrational manoeuvres of real driving. SPART closes that gap by synthesising geometrically exact 2-D LiDAR point clouds directly from recorded trajectories, with no graphical simulator, no GPU and no manual scene design. This is achieved using three geometric optimisations: a range-gated temporal eligibility scheduler, an angular interval filter and a Numba JIT-compiled scan kernel.
